# MATH 4820 (Cross-Listed: CS 6820) — Computational Image Forensics, Inverse Problems, and Visual Privacy

An interdisciplinary educational and research-oriented project examining the mathematics of digital image reconstruction, forensic image analysis, and visual privacy engineering. Originally developed from the [source Gist](https://gist.github.com/topquark22/4db7770e3ca7488cb5ae56afb558c593), the project now comprises a full draft university curriculum and the foundation of a future textbook and companion workbook.

The central theme is the acquisition, loss, reconstruction, and protection of information: what an observation actually contains, what can be recovered reliably, what is inferred or synthesized by a model, and what constitutes defensible privacy protection.

## Project goals

- Develop a mathematically rigorous, interdisciplinary fourth-year undergraduate / graduate cross-listed university course.
- Develop the lecture material into a coherent scientific textbook, accompanied by a separate workbook of mathematical and computational exercises.
- Provide reproducible Python examples, laboratory infrastructure, and documented sample datasets for classroom instruction and independent study.
- Integrate mathematical proofs, numerical experiments, scientific validation, and critical assessment of forensic and privacy claims.
- Preserve reproducible illustrations, source attribution, and clear distinctions between theoretical guarantees and empirical findings.

## Curriculum

| Part | Weeks | Focus |
|------|-------|-------|
| I — Geometric Foundations | 1–3 | Discrete images, interpolation, geometric transformations, and compression |
| II — Inverse Problems | 4–7 | Fourier analysis, convolution, ill-posedness, regularization, and reconstruction |
| III — Forensic Standards | 8–10 | Integrity, reproducibility, scientific validation, and learned restoration |
| IV — Privacy Engineering | 11–14 | Information leakage, redaction, generative anonymization, and privacy–utility evaluation |

## Draft status

The first curriculum manuscript draft is complete: 28 lecture documents across 14 weeks, 112 workbook exercises with instructor answer sketches, six laboratory specifications, a TA laboratory manual, and a capstone specification. Mathematical illustrations and their generator sources are included.

Further development includes executable Python laboratories and sample datasets, automated numerical tests, the midterm examination and associated data, expanded solutions, independent mathematical and bibliographic review, workload evaluation, and editorial preparation for publication.

The course designation, cross-listing, credits, prerequisites, term, assessment arrangements, and institutional policies are proposed rather than formally approved. The material is intended for development and independent study; it has not yet undergone institutional approval or independent academic validation.

Public reference material is not confidential assessment material. Withheld test references, private records, and examination answer keys must be maintained separately for formal delivery. Third-party media remain subject to their respective licenses and attribution requirements.
